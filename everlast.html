Life in the Everlast

Life in the Everlast

What it is like to live in the greatest city in the world, and what everyone in it is reaching for.

How the Everlast began

The Shining Everlast was founded nine thousand years ago, a millennium after the Sundering, and named to last forever. Most of the elven kingdoms, and most of the other races, had been wiped out. The elves who survived built the Everlast, and ruled it directly, as an elves-only empire, for thousands of years.

Then, about a hundred and twenty years ago, in the same years as the Great Umbrasol Siege, they opened the gates and handed governance to a Senate shared by every race. The story the Everlast likes to tell is of an elven warrior king, a true hero, who chose to share rule rather than hoard it, and that choice is what built the flourishing city of many peoples that exists today. Officially, the reason for the handover is unknown. Some say the elves simply grew tired of ruling.

They remain the true rulers of this empire. They can take it back whenever they like, and I would not forget it.

Paraphrased from Professor Linfay's lecture on the founding of the Everlast

Leaving your mark

The Everlast has its own version of a national dream, and every citizen grows up with it: to leave your mark. It is meant literally. Folk belief holds that great acts, discoveries and hard-won knowledge leave a permanent trace in the living Mana Field, and that a life lived well is one the field remembers.

Each calling has its own road to it.

Mages, engineers and artificers
Invent and discover.
Bards
Immortalize stories and music.
Warriors
Earn deeds worth retelling.
Druids
Quietly keep the Mana Field and the natural world healthy, and seek no glory for it.
Rangers
Close cousins of the druids, devoted to preserving wildlife.
Rogues
Take the path in reverse, and become a legend whispered through secrecy and rumor.
Clerics
Spread faith and shared joy until they are remembered almost as saints.
Everyone else
Leave their mark in small ways: pride in their craft and steady improvement. The best bread in the district. A shop that becomes a neighborhood fixture. A stretch of street kept clean and beautiful for forty years.

Magic and machinery

Magic did not replace technology in the Everlast. The two grew up together. The street lamps run on mana, but real engineers built them. The printing presses are mechanical, but the ink is enchanted. Mana rivers and currents are visible in the sky above the towers.

At street level, the Ground Bazaar sprawls for dozens of miles across countless stacked floors. Monorail lines run between and through the towers. Faerie workers run guild wards and keep the city's upkeep going, the way they do in the Fey Lords' own realms.

Not everything is magic for its own sake. Edward Notch, a famous womanizer, decided that women wanted magic to make them more beautiful and that nobody was making magical makeup, although several women were already trying. He and a few friends launched Maiden Fairay, the first magical cosmetic, and it turned some women into models overnight. His company is now one of the ten largest in the empire, especially since the military began using its products.

Public spellcasting in the capital is licensed and logged under Harrow's Law.

Food

Everlast food comes in two tiers. Everyday food is grown and processed at scale by magic: magical furnaces, plant magic, cheap and fast and everywhere. Artisan food, made by chefs who treat cooking as their way of leaving a mark, is a weekend or special-occasion indulgence. Picture bread that bakes itself in mid-air and lands finished. The gap between the two is a quiet class line that everyone notices.

Magic itself is a flavor in the Everlast, in the way spice or heat is elsewhere. A good dish delivers a burst of mana along with its taste, and food lovers compare dishes by their mana intensity as much as their flavor.

The Hearthside Manamelt

The national dish is a rustic stew in which every ingredient is enchanted separately with its own mana flavor, so the whole tastes harmonious while each spoonful still stands out. An enchanted broth carries a tangy, spicy mana. A root vegetable grounds it, a leafy green freshens it, a mushroom gives it texture, and the meat brings a smoky mana burst. It is topped, the way an onion soup would be, with a layer of melted enchanted cheese that faintly shimmers. Every home has its version, and the best chefs serve a grand one, and both count.

The four festivals

Marking Day

The festival of the national dream. Everyone publicly shows what they built, discovered or achieved that year, from new inventions to a single perfect cake.

The Illuminance

A blazing festival of lights everywhere, built around giving, kindness and making others feel loved. Every layer of society contributes. Guilds hunt rare creatures and gather special ingredients for the feast. Mages and warriors put on shows. The council lights and hosts the great central structure. Ordinary citizens host guests and give, to family and to strangers alike.

Unity Week

For one week, factions and classes trade places. Apprentices and family members move between factions; the wealthy and the poor cross over. A noble sweeps a street while someone from a poorer district shadows how a manor or merchant house actually runs. The point is real appreciation for what each group contributes, and the week ends with tournaments, competitions and the formal renewal of alliances.

Not everyone takes part in good faith. Some show up briefly, for the public relations, and leave, and that breeds a certain cynicism about the whole thing.

Founding Day

The anniversary of the Everlast's founding. Everyone gets the day off, fireworks and parties break out across the towers, and the elves descend from their towers to give a speech about the founding and about gratitude. It ends looking forward: charitable giving, and new public works announced for the year ahead.
