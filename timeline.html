Timeline

Timeline

Ten thousand years across five ages, from the round world to the Fourth Age.

History in Mirrorbloom is counted from the Sundering, about ten thousand years ago. Open any age to read it.

The Primordial Era

Before the Sundering, more than ten thousand years agoBefore the Sundering, the world was round.
The beginning of record

The First Dawn

The earliest moment any historical record attempts to describe. The theologian Arven Solis wrote in her Commentaries on Absence that the First Dawn was not the beginning of light but the beginning of the awareness that light existed, which is a different and more significant thing. Her work is required reading at the Mages College and considered heresy by three of the four major religious orders.

The long middle

The Age of Unity

The period in which a single empire is believed to have stood at its height. Architecture from this time, recognizable by stone foundations curved for a round world, survives in fragments across Solance. The largest intact structure is the Vault of Aetherin in the Greenbelt Spires of the Shining Everlast, which predates the empire around it by thousands of years and which no one has excavated past the third sub-level.

Before the Sundering

The Library of Hope

The Library of Hope, called different things in different lands, is older than the Sundering. It is open to all and fairly safe, and yet expeditions that go deep inside never come back. Many believe the answers about the Sundering lie somewhere within it.

Before the Sundering

The Two Hands

The oldest surviving myth, preserved in at least eleven cultures, tells of the Two Hands of the World: two aspects of a single being who kept the world in balance by never letting go of each other. The versions do not agree on what happened when they let go. Scholars note that the disagreement is itself informative.

The last years

Signs before the Sundering

Fragments describe growing instability: storms with no cause, animals fleeing without direction, a quality of silence before dawn that people found worth writing down. The historian Brek Ashvalen called it the world holding its breath. And one record, the only record of a true omen anyone has ever found, describes a red moon three years before the Sundering. Nothing else from that time survives, which is exactly why some scholars refuse to trust it.

About ten thousand years ago

The Sundering

The world split. The round became flat, and the single became two: Solance above, Umbrasol below. The goddess divided into the Great Hope and the Great Despair, and the Fey Lords were born from the moment of breaking, six pairs, each splintered into a bright half and a shadow. Everything before is measured as before the Sundering; everything after is counted by age.

Named figures

Arven Solis
Theologian, author of Commentaries on Absence, the most cited and most argued-with text on the Primordial Era. Died still writing the fourth volume.
Brek Ashvalen
Historian who coined the phrase "the world holding its breath" and spent the last decade of his career trying to live it down. His actual scholarship on pre-Sundering archaeology is foundational.

The First Age

The Age of Ash, from the Sundering to about nine thousand years agoThe world that existed after the Sundering was not the world that existed before it.
Years 1 to 100

The Scattering

The survivors found themselves on a flat world with none of the infrastructure, physical, political or spiritual, that had sustained them. Estimates for the surviving population run from forty thousand to two hundred thousand across every people. Mountains, rivers and coastlines bore no relation to anything they had known.

Year 80

The first doorway is opened

The first recorded contact with the Fey Lords. Survivors in what is now the Greenbelt Spires found a doorway to the First Morning, the Dawnward King's realm, and came back. Their account, the Document of Seven Steps, is the oldest first-hand description of a Fey Lord, who was described as enormous, golden, and entirely unable to tell them how long they had been gone.

Years 100 to 300

The Unnamed Extinctions

At least thirty peoples of the old world died out. Some in the Scattering, some in the warlord conflicts that followed, some simply declined. The Mirhen are the best documented. The Solvari, the Ashenkind and the Pale Weavers are known only by name. The rest are archaeology.

Year 150 onward

The warlords

As survivors found each other, conflict followed the resources. The first named warlord is Gorrath the Unsatisfied, a self-given title, whose forty-year campaign for the eastern water sources ended at the hands of his own lieutenants. The warlord centuries that followed were brutal and locally effective, and the warlords, whatever else they did, protected the food.

Year 900

The Covenant of the Eleven

Eleven warlords of the central plains agreed to stop fighting and start trading. The covenant lasted sixty years, which is remarkable given the circumstances. Three of its signatories founded the first proto-kingdoms. The original document survives in the Gilded Goose's restricted archive.

About nine thousand years ago

The first empires

A millennium after the Sundering, the surviving elves founded the Shining Everlast and named it to last forever. Captain Reagan founded the Yenfin Empire in the same era; which of the two came first is still argued. In the same age lived Archmage Violet T. Fernand, the magic anomaly who became an archmage at five and shaped how the world understands magic to this day.

Named figures

Gorrath the Unsatisfied
The first named warlord. Self-titled. Killed by his own lieutenants. Used as a cautionary example in military academies.
The Seven Signatories
The survivors who signed the Document of Seven Steps: Arrath, Solvaine, Gresk, Mira of the Shore, Tollen, Fess, and the one recorded only as "the one who would not give their name."
Violet T. Fernand
The first anomaly anyone names. Entire fields of study exist only to decode her writing.

The Second Age

The Age of Kingdoms, from about nine thousand years ago to about five hundred years agoSurvival was possible. The Second Age is what Solance chose to become.
Early in the age

The Great Founding

Most of the age's kingdoms were founded, many from the late proto-kingdoms. The most famous founding is the Kingdom of Vel, established by twin sisters Velara and Velsin, whose disagreement about governance produced two kingdoms sharing one name for sixty years until their grandchildren reunified them.

Early in the age

The first maps of a flat world

The cartographer Thessaly Vorn produced the first surviving map that treated Solance's flatness as fact. Her maps are still the basis of modern cartography, and her companion work, The Shape of the Below, speculating about Umbrasol, was considered eccentric at the time and prescient now.

Early in the age

The First Mages' Compact

Seven of the largest kingdoms agreed to share arcane research and forbid the weaponization of certain kinds of magic. It lasted four hundred years, the longest multilateral agreement of the age, and its principles still shape magical law.

Mid age

The University of the Still Lake

The oldest continuously operating academic institution in Solance, founded by the scholar-king Areth Voss, who refused the title of king and asked to be called the first student. His chair at the head of the faculty table is still left empty.

About six thousand years ago

The Age of the Warring Kingdoms

Centuries of war between kingdoms, in which geography became power. The Cartography Wars belong to this period: a conflict over the right to draw official maps, because whoever controlled the maps controlled trade routes, borders and access to the doorways. In this age, too, Angelia V. Martin, grand priestess of Hope, became immortal. She has never said how.

Late in the age

The Great Theological Debate

Twelve kingdoms hosted a formal debate on the nature of the Fey Lords and their relationship to the goddesses. It reached no consensus but produced the Twelve Positions, and every faith today traces its lineage to one of them, even the ones that claim not to.

Two to three thousand years ago

The wellspring

The Kenanism tradition takes shape around its mana leyline. Its pilgrims begin to found new bloodlines across the world, and Mirrorbloom starts its long journey from ten races to hundreds.

About two thousand years ago

The Brumas

Cannibal witches called the Brumas develop biomancy, keeping their prey alive and growing extra limbs on them to feed the tribe. The empires will wipe them out fifteen hundred years later and keep what they knew.

About seven hundred years ago

The Glasswrights

Sandra P. Won mutates herself in Kenanism and settles on the outskirts of the still elves-only Everlast. Her family becomes a kingdom ruled by her descendants.

The last centuries of the age

The first Umbrasol incursions

Small groups from Umbrasol appear at the borders of seven kingdoms at once, kill, withdraw and return. The kingdoms respond one by one, which is to say inadequately. Unification attempts follow and fail, until Queen Taveth the Clear-Eyed of Solmere issues the Solmere Warning: the threat her people had called a problem for their children had arrived, and their children were them now.

About five hundred years ago

Native vampires

Vampires arise out of Kenanism: more mutated than the foreign kind, missing many of their weaknesses, and contemptuous of the outsiders who share their name.

Named figures

Velara and Velsin
Twin founders of the Kingdom of Vel. A standard case study in founding conflicts.
Areth Voss
Scholar-king and founder of the University of the Still Lake.
Taveth the Clear-Eyed
Last queen of Solmere, author of the Solmere Warning. She died defending her capital in the first week of the invasion.
Angelia V. Martin
Grand priestess of Hope and the oldest immortal on record.

The Third Age

The Age of Shadows, from about five hundred years ago to the Great Umbrasol Siege, a hundred and twenty years agoUmbrasol did not announce itself. It arrived.
Year 1

The First Night

A coordinated invasion of Solance through hundreds of crossing points at once, with no warning. The incursions of the previous centuries had been reconnaissance, not raids. Solmere fell in the first week. Queen Taveth's last recorded words, preserved by a surviving guard, were "I said so."

The first century

The falling kingdoms

Two hundred kingdoms fell. Some were destroyed, some absorbed, some simply stopped functioning as their people fled toward higher ground. The University of the Still Lake was destroyed and rebuilt three times. Around the same time the empires finally wiped out the Brumas.

Early in the age

The first understanding of Umbrasol

The scholar Pressa of Vel, working from captured documents and prisoners, wrote the first academic description of Umbrasol and the Law of Reflection. Hers was the first text to call the relationship a reflection, and the first to suggest that fighting Umbrasol with pure force was like punching a mirror and being surprised by the cuts. She was killed before she could publish a follow-up.

Through the war

The Fey Lords speak

Under the pressure of the invasion, the Hearthkeeper and the Hunter King, acting independently, gave Solance's surviving kingdoms information about their own shadow twins. They shared tactics, and declined to explain the Law of Reflection or the Sundering. Why they drew the line there has never been established.

Through the war

The Long Resistance and the Council of Sixty

Decade after decade of holding action. The surviving kingdoms, reduced to about sixty, formed a single defensive council under its first Warden, the warrior-queen Solvaine Arrath. The cost in lives is estimated in the tens of millions, numbers that lose their meaning at scale, which historians say is the point.

Through the war

The Amber Field

A combined force under General Toven Rek, twenty-three years old, defeated a major Umbrasol advance and held the line for the first time in a generation. She lived to ninety-one and is buried on the field. Her manual, The Hard Way, is still required reading.

Through the war

The Reflection Strategy and the Great Sealing

The arcane researcher Coris Vane found a way to turn the Law of Reflection against the invaders with mirror-light techniques, and it turned the war. Then a coalition of mages worked for eleven years to seal most of Umbrasol's crossing points. The seal still holds, though scholars argue about whether it is weakening. The mages who performed it did not survive it. Their names are on the Wall of the Sealed in the Everlast's Crownspires.

About two hundred and fifty years ago

Crockett arrives

A goblin, already very old by any goblin's reckoning, arrives through a doorway from another realm entirely, carrying a history no one here knows and a conviction that existing institutions are failing the people inside them. He founds the Gilded Goose. The city's first official letter of concern arrives roughly three weeks later, and it will not be the last.

A hundred and twenty years ago

The Great Umbrasol Siege

The largest invasion of all. Umbrasol besieged the Shining Everlast. Kelvarion sent reinforcements and flooded the invaders out, a debt the Everlast has never repaid. Yenfin fought as a hero and entered its golden age. Vaelgard, nearly overrun for the fifth time, armed every citizen and became one of the six great key points of the defense. The bard John Fevor brought dragons. Vesper Nightfall and Isolde gave aid. Crockett fought. The Glasswright kingdom fell, and its survivors fled into the Everlast. Those who lived through it still call it the War.

A hundred and twenty years ago

The Great Repulsion

The final push drove Umbrasol's forces back below. It was not a complete victory, and the threat is permanent, but it was enough to end the age. In the same years the elves of the Everlast opened their gates and handed governance to a Senate shared by every race.

Named figures

Pressa of Vel
First to describe Umbrasol academically and to call it a reflection.
Solvaine Arrath
First Warden of the Council of Sixty. Her descendants are a major noble family in the modern Everlast.
Toven Rek
The twenty-three-year-old general of the Amber Field.
Coris Vane
Developer of the Reflection Strategy.
John Fevor
Bard, dragon diplomat, and later founder of the Dragon Layer.

The Fourth Age

The Age of Empires, from the Great Repulsion to the present dayThe Fourth Age began with the Repulsion and has not yet ended.
Year 1

The Senate

The Shining Everlast's Senate takes up governance. Its first Speaker, Arren of the Long Table, chose his name to honor the Hearthkeeper, whose court stood by Solance through the war. The Senate's founding charter requires every faction to be represented regardless of military strength.

Year 5

The Dragon Layer

John Fevor founds the Everlast's bard academy, under a name that is not a misspelling.

Early in the age

The Rebuilding

The war had reduced the built world to a fraction of what it had been. Libraries came first: the loss of knowledge was considered the most dangerous long-term wound of the invasion.

The age's middle years

The Doorway Atlas

A joint commission produced the first comprehensive map of known Fey Lord doorways in Solance. It has been revised many times and is now maintained by the Gilded Goose's Scroll division in partnership with the Mages College.

The age's middle years

The Long Peace Treaty

The nations of Solance agreed a system of arbitration, shared standards for Umbrasol incursions, and the principle that no nation may seal or open a Fey Lord doorway on its own. The Everlast's Senate was the last to ratify it, by one vote.

Fifty years ago

Hopelight

A rabbitfolk named Hopkins discovers what Hopelight crystal can really do. Within a generation it powers the entire Everlast.

Twenty years ago

The isolation shields

A halfling revolt infects the Everlast's Hopelight network with necromancy. District isolation shields are installed so it can never spread that way again.

Recent years

Harrow's Law

Public spellcasting in the capital is licensed and logged. Crime falls. Resentment rises.

The present

The current day

The Everlast is short of Hopelight and its Senate is deadlocked. Despair cults are rising across Solance at historic levels. More children are being born with empathy and stranger gifts every year. And the Gilded Goose has just found something in the ruins beneath Yenfin.

Named figures

Arren of the Long Table
First Speaker of the Senate of the Shining Everlast.
Hopkins
The rabbitfolk who unlocked Hopelight.
