Cosmology

Cosmology

One flat world, split top and bottom like a reflection on still water.

Mirrorbloom is a single world, not two. Picture a coin. The upper face, Solance, is whimsical, beautiful fantasy, a little Alice in Wonderland around the edges. The lower face, Umbrasol, is its exact dark mirror: pure horror. They are not separate planes so much as two sides of the same thing, held together by one law that governs everything else in the setting.

Above, thirteen nations share three continents, a floating dominion and the deep kingdoms under the sea (see The Thirteen Nations). Below, every one of them is cast again, with the same names and the same peoples, corrupted. Umbrasol's sky holds two red moons. Everyone below knows whose eyes they are.

At the rim of Solance the land simply stops. Scholars call it the Boundary: a beautiful, unsettling edge where the ground gives way to open space and, somewhere beneath, Umbrasol. Gravity weakens the closer you get. People have tried again and again to explore that space and build colonies out in it, and every single attempt has been attacked and ruined by Umbrasol, even expeditions launched from the far side of the world. Nobody has explained how Umbrasol always knows. It has never stopped anyone from trying.

Most worlds are spheres, and most have no Law of Reflection at all. The Sundering is what made ours what it is. Realm walkers, who cross between worlds as easily as you cross a street, avoid Mirrorbloom like the plague, and none of them will say why. I have a suspicion it is connected to the other thing we do not like to say in class: that Mirrorbloom's own people cannot leave.

Paraphrased from Professor Linfay's recorded lectures at the Mages College

The Law of Reflection

Beauty and goodness generated above directly feed horror and evil below. It is not metaphorical and it is not optional. It is closer to a law of physics than a moral teaching. A kindness performed in Mirrorbloom is, somewhere beneath it, paid for.

The law reads intent, not deeds. A kindness performed for show moves nothing; a genuine change of heart moves the whole scale. That is why the old saying holds that true goodness is never free. Even the dead are not exempt: each soul is judged on its own, but genuine goodness in one life quietly pushes its mirror self toward a darker fate.

The Shackle

Everyone born in Mirrorbloom is bound to a mirror self, a counterpart in the other half whose alignment runs opposite to their own and is inversely linked. Scholars of the Shining Everlast call this binding the Shackle. Most people live and die without ever meeting theirs. Some spend their whole lives trying to. A few, the unlucky ones, are found first.

When one half of a shackled pair truly changes, in intent rather than in act, the other half shifts to keep the balance. Goodness earned above darkens something below. Cruelty below brightens something above. No one has ever broken a Shackle, and it is not known what would happen if someone did.

Studying the Law of Reflection itself is outlawed. Most people never think about why. For the deeper layers of what is actually known, and by whom, see the Knowledge Iceberg.

The Breaking

Before any of this, there was no above and no below. There was only the Great Hope and the Great Despair, two halves of a single radiant whole, though no living thing remembers what that whole was called, or whether it had a name at all.

Once, there was one being, whole, or two who were as one. Something happened between them that no one now remembers, or no one was ever told. The world split in the breaking: above, joy, untouched and bright. Below, despair, sharp-toothed and starving. The one above does not know what became of the one below. The one below has convinced herself something was stolen, a crown, a right, a throne, because that is easier to survive believing than the truth: that she is simply, completely alone.The oldest telling, as the Archfey's courts repeat it

The cause of the split is, even in the world, a genuine mystery. No scholar, court or scripture agrees on why it happened, and that uncertainty is part of the setting's design. What is known for certain: the Great Hope does not remember what became of the Great Despair, and does not know what her shadow now believes was taken from her. The Great Despair has built an entire identity, an entire crusade, around reclaiming a crown that never existed.

Historians call the same event the Sundering and date everything from it. It happened roughly ten thousand years ago. Before it, some scholars argue, the world was round. See the Timeline.

The three tiers of the divine

Mirrorbloom's divine order has three tiers, and the difference between them matters to anyone who prays, bargains or picks a fight.

First tier
The Great Hope and the Great Despair. They are creators, not expressions of the Mana Field, and nothing else in the world is their equal.
Second tier
The twelve Archfey aspects. This is the everyday pantheon, the gods people actually pray to, swear by and leave offerings for.
Third tier
The High Fey. Lesser divinities who hold the domains the twelve do not.

The High Fey

A High Fey can come into being three ways. Some are born or spawned. Some are generated by strong belief or by ritual, sustained long enough to take shape. And some are made: a mortal of great power raised up by an Archfey, or one who climbs there by sheer power alone.

High Fey normally sit well below the Archfey in strength, but not always. A rare, exceptional High Fey could in principle rival an Archfey, or even overthrow one. Nobody can say whether that has ever happened, and the question hangs over every ambitious mortal who has ever wondered how far up they could climb.

New domains of worship land in this tier rather than among the twelve. Magic, knowledge and fate, trade and fortune, death and endings: these are all spoken of, but which High Fey hold them is not yet set down in this record.

The Kenanism Empire follows a faith entirely its own and does not belong to this pantheon.

The Great Hope, She Who Does Not Know, is the All Mother of Solance. The Great Despair, the Uncrowned Queen, is Mistress Despair to Umbrasol, and the red moons are her eyes.

The Archfey

The Six Great Fey Lords did not witness the Breaking. They were born from it, the way light scatters into a spectrum when it passes through broken glass. Each Lord exists as a mirrored pair: a bright aspect above and a corrupted shadow below, twelve aspects in all, each holding a named court. Only the Fey Lords know the truth of what happened during the Breaking, and the Great Hope has been sealed away from that knowledge.

Each Archfey keeps a smaller, personal afterlife for their most devoted followers, flavored by that Archfey's domain. None of them rivals the Sleeping Meadows in size.

The Archfey also do one thing that most mortals prefer not to think about. When an anomaly is born, a person with almost limitless potential who grows faster the more they use it, the Archfey or the empires kill it in the cradle. The reason is the last anomaly to survive into adulthood. He killed four Archfey before the All Mother struck him down herself.

Crossing between halves

Each god and each Archfey aspect holds a realm of their own, fourteen in total, tied closely to Mirrorbloom but standing outside it and reachable only through doorways. Some doorways are fixed and well mapped, marked on old charts and guarded or worshipped accordingly. Others wander, opening and closing without warning, in places that held nothing the morning before.

Through these doors, an Archfey may pull a being out into Mirrorbloom, or push a piece of their own realm into the world: a patch of ground that simply becomes, for a time or forever, somewhere else entirely. See Courts & Realms.

Crossing between Solance and Umbrasol themselves is another matter. The halves were not meant to touch, and the places where the boundary runs thin are the most dangerous ground in the world. Ancient ruins have a way of sitting on exactly that kind of ground.
