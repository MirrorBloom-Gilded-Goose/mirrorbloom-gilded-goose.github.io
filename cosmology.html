The Foundation

Cosmology

One flat world, split top and bottom like a reflection on still water.

Mirrorbloom is a single world, not two. Picture a disc, or a coin — the upper face, Solance, is whimsical, beautiful, Alice-coded fantasy. The lower face, Umbrasol, is its exact dark mirror: pure horror. They are not separate planes so much as two sides of the same thing, bound by a single law that governs everything else in this setting.

The Law of Reflection

Beauty and goodness generated above directly feeds horror and evil below. It is not metaphorical and it is not optional — it is closer to a law of physics than a moral teaching. A kindness performed in Mirrorbloom is, somewhere beneath it, paid for.

Above — Solance

Fifteen empires, whimsical and Alice-coded. Joy, hope, beauty, and abundance, generated by mortal lives lived well.

Below — Umbrasol

Fifteen corrupted twin empires — same names, same peoples, inverted. Every empire above casts an exact shadow below.

The law cuts deeper than nations. Intent drives alignment: a genuine change of heart in one half of the world mirrors across to the other, whether either party wants it to or not.

The Shackle System

Everyone born in Mirrorbloom is bound to a mirror self — a counterpart in the other half whose alignment runs opposite to their own and is inversely linked. Scholars of the Shining Everlast call this binding the Shackle. Most people live and die without ever meeting theirs. Some spend their whole lives trying to. A few, the unlucky ones, are found first.

When one half of a shackled pair changes — truly changes, in intent rather than in act — the other half shifts to keep the balance. Goodness earned above darkens something below. Cruelty below brightens something above. No one has ever broken a Shackle. It is not known what would happen if someone did.

The Three Layers of Magic

The personal core. Every living thing carries its own reservoir of mana — small in most, vast in a few, shaped by temperament as much as training.

The ambient field. Mana saturates the world itself, an ocean both halves swim in. Casters draw on the field to fuel workings their core alone could never sustain.

Field influence. The rarest tier. Beings of sufficient power can inject their own magic into the field, bending ambient reality around them — weather that obeys a mood, ground that remembers a grudge. The gods and the Archfey live at this tier. A small number of mortals have touched it. Most regretted it.

The Breaking

Before any of this, there was no above and no below — there was only the Great Hope and the Great Despair, two halves of a single radiant whole, though no living thing remembers what that whole was called, or whether it had a name at all.

Once, there was one being, whole — or two who were as one. Something happened between them that no one now remembers, or no one was ever told. The world split in the breaking: above, joy, untouched and bright. Below, despair, sharp-toothed and starving. The one above does not know what became of the one below. The one below has convinced herself something was stolen — a crown, a right, a throne — because that is easier to survive believing than the truth: that she is simply, completely alone.

The cause of the split is, even in-world, a genuine mystery — no scholar, court, or scripture agrees on why it happened, and that uncertainty is itself part of the setting's design. What's known for certain: the Great Hope does not remember what became of the Great Despair, and does not know what her shadow now believes was taken from her. The Great Despair, for her part, has built an entire identity, an entire crusade, around reclaiming a crown that never existed.

The Archfey — The Six Great Fey Lords

The Six Great Fey Lords were not witnesses to the Breaking — they were born from it, the way light scatters into a spectrum when it passes through broken glass. Each Lord exists as a mirrored pair: a bright aspect above and a corrupted shadow below, twelve aspects in all, each holding a named court. Only the Fey Lords know the truth of what happened during the Breaking — and the Great Hope has been sealed away from that knowledge.

Each pair below is shown as the world holds them: the bright aspect beside the shadow it feeds.

Crossing Between Halves

Each god and each Fey Lord aspect holds a realm of their own — fourteen in total — tied closely to Mirrorbloom but standing outside it, reachable only through doorways. Some doorways are fixed and well-mapped, marked on old charts and guarded or worshipped accordingly. Others wander, opening and closing without warning, in places that held nothing the morning before.

Through these doors, a Fey Lord may pull a being out into Mirrorbloom, or push a piece of their own realm into the world — a patch of ground that simply becomes, for a time or forever, somewhere else entirely.
