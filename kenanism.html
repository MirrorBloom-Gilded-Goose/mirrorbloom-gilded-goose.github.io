The Kenanism Empire

The Kenanism Empire

Where change is holy, and the hundred races of Mirrorbloom began.

Kenanism shares Solmarch with the Shining Everlast, and it is arguably the most unusual nation in Mirrorbloom, and the one with the greatest impact on everyone else. Its whole culture, and its whole religion, is built on a single conviction: that mutation is sacred.

The faith of change

Mana exposure mutates living things. Almost every other nation outlaws doing it to yourself on purpose. Kenanism does the opposite. The empire is built around a mana leyline, a wellspring of raw power, and its people freely expose themselves to it, along with their food and their wildlife, all in pursuit of one goal: the perfect being.

The tradition is between two and three thousand years old. Access is open: anyone may come and attempt a mutation. The guards around the wellspring are there only to protect it from sabotage, not to decide who is worthy. Kenanism is entirely its own religion, separate from the pantheon of the goddesses and the Archfey.

The results are not always kind. Some pilgrims deliberately expose their children to the wellspring, or to one of the ancient ley line wells that Kenanism guards, hoping for a gift. Usually what the child receives is harm.

The pilgrimage

When a pilgrim's mutation satisfies them, custom says they leave. They go out into the world and found a new bloodline somewhere else. This is the true origin of the hundreds of distinct races across Mirrorbloom. The original ten races were natural. Every other one traces back to a Kenanism pilgrim line.

Some lines are thousands of years old. Some are young: the Glasswrights appeared only seven hundred years ago, and Mirrorbloom's native vampires came out of Kenanism about five hundred years ago.

Keeping the empire whole

An empire that sends its most successful people away should hollow itself out. Kenanism solves this in two ways. First, native-born citizens must have children, ordinary unmutated children, before they are permitted to undertake their own pilgrimage. Second, the pilgrim bloodlines come home. Once a line founded elsewhere grows populous enough, its descendants return to the capital and carve out a faction of their own. Kenanism today is a competitive patchwork of races and factions, each descended from a different successful mutation.

The throne

The throne does not pass by simple succession. When a ruling emperor or empress is overthrown, the contending families and bloodlines must prove their superiority through a series of trials. There is never a single duel, because one fight is too much a matter of luck to show real fitness; the trials test many traits in turn. Whoever takes the throne has proven, in Kenanism's eyes, that their line is closer to the perfect being than any other.

The system has a flaw it has never fixed. Families sometimes simply eliminate rival bloodlines out of ambition, and in doing so cut away talent the empire might have needed later. The competition that drives Kenanism forward also keeps tripping it.

How the world sees it

Other nations are wary of Kenanism, mostly of what its military could become, but none of them are hostile. Its pilgrims are everywhere, its descendants are everyone, and almost every nation in the world owes some part of its population to the wellspring.
