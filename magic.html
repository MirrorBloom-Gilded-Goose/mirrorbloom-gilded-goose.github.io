Magic

Magic

How power works in Mirrorbloom, and what every use of it costs.

Magic in Mirrorbloom works in three layers. Every caster uses the first, most use the second, and almost nobody survives long at the third.

The three layers

1. The personal core

Every living being generates its own magic in a personal core: small in most, vast in a few. The core works everywhere, even where nothing else does, and it refills on its own. It is shaped by temperament as much as training, so magic takes the color of the soul that holds it. Crockett's core runs to chaos and time. Cherry's turns the air a different color and brings carnival music with it. Will's biomancy glows green, and when he spends his own blood on it, it mists out red shot through with green. No two casters' magic looks quite the same, even when they cast the same spell.

2. The Mana Field

Mana saturates the world itself: an ambient field that both halves swim in. Casters draw on the Mana Field to fuel workings their core alone could never sustain, the way a mill draws on a river. The field is thicker in some places than others: dense over ley lines, thin over old battlefields, strange beyond description near doorways.

The field is also, to some degree, aware. It is not a mind the way a person is a mind, but it reacts, it remembers, and it is semi-conscious. Everlast folk belief holds that great acts leave a permanent trace in it (see leaving your mark).

3. Field influence

The rarest layer. Beings of sufficient power do not merely draw on the field: they inject their own magic into it at scale, and reality shifts around them. Weather that obeys a mood. Ground that remembers a grudge. A guildhall that is larger inside than out. The gods and the Archfey live here permanently. A handful of mortals have touched it, and most of them regretted it.

Deliberately injecting magic into the field by ritual is illegal across Solance. Most people know that much. Very few know why. See the Knowledge Iceberg.

Dead zones

A dead zone is not an absence of magic. It is an anti-magic field with something like awareness of its own, and it actively zaps away cast magic inside it. Spells fizzle, enchantments go quiet, and fields of influence simply stop. The one thing it cannot touch is the personal core, which keeps refilling, so a caster who walks out of a dead zone has their magic back almost at once. The trouble is only ever what happens while they are inside.

Knowledge is the real power

Raw magical output does not decide who wins a fight. Skill does: knowing how to wield what you have, and how to exploit what the other side has. A modest caster who understands their power will beat a reservoir of mana with no idea how to aim it, and that is the logic behind how dangerous anyone in this setting actually is.

It goes further. The deeper someone truly understands how the world works, the Mana Field, the Archfey, the goddesses, the more precisely they can bend it, and they grow stronger as a side effect of comprehension. That understanding always costs something and always changes the person who reaches it. What it changes them into is their own choice. Ignorance is the comfortable default; once someone learns enough truth, they decide how to carry it. Wonder and resilience pull toward Hope. Crushing nihilism pulls toward Despair.

History first, fireball later. The more we learn about the world, the more we can manipulate it to our whims.

Paraphrased from Professor Linfay's first recorded lecture at the Mages College

Who uses it

Wizards
Small internal reserves and a great deal of intelligence. They stockpile spells so they always have an answer for everything.
Witches
Much the same as wizards, but focused on ritual and the darker arts.
Clerics
Power drawn from faith and from their god.
Sorcerers
The lucky ones: extraordinary reserves and good instincts, which is exactly why they attract so much jealousy and mockery.

A new kind of magic is discovered almost every year. Very few catch on, either because nobody wants to learn something new when the old methods work, or because the new kind simply is not very good.

Harrow's Law

In the capital of the Shining Everlast, casting magic in public requires a license, and every single casting must be logged, so that nobody does anything illegal or harmful unseen. For now the law covers the city only, but the Senate is pushing to make it empire wide and is in talks with other nations to adopt it.

A minority gets away with ignoring it through connections, and a crackdown is under way. Crime has dropped tremendously since it came into force. Dissatisfaction with it grows every day.

Hopelight

Hopelight is a crystal that absorbs mana and releases it better than any other known material, and it is the fuel the Shining Everlast runs on: its lamps, its monorails, its power grids and its industry. For most of history it was used for fancy lamps and small mana batteries. Its real properties were discovered only fifty years ago, by a rabbitfolk named Hopkins, and the Everlast still leads the world in Hopelight research and production.

What makes it extraordinary is that crystals share a frequency. Join one crystal to another and the effect multiplies tenfold for each crystal added, so a whole district's Hopelight behaves as one field rather than many lamps. That is its strength and its danger: whatever happens to one crystal eventually happens to all of them. District isolation shields were installed twenty years ago, after a halfling revolt infected the system with necromancy, and they exist to keep any infection contained.

Most of it is made by the Foundry. Right now there is not enough of it. The Hopelight Crisis is a plain supply shortage: the city needs more than can be produced, and the Senate has spent years failing to agree on what to do. See the Shining Everlast.

Mutation

Exposure to concentrated mana changes living things. Most nations outlaw doing it to yourself on purpose. The Kenanism Empire does the opposite and built a religion on it, and thousands of years of its pilgrims are why Mirrorbloom has hundreds of races instead of the original ten.

Overloading the body

Some people, usually on the edge of death, try to force more mana into their bodies than they can hold. It works, briefly: strength rises, and a flooded brain thinks faster, which can win a fight or buy a few more minutes alive. Going past your limits almost always kills you or mutates you, and the mutation is almost never good. A few cases end in new powers or permanent strength. Out of a million recorded attempts, two have succeeded.

Biomancy

Biomancy, the magic of flesh, was developed about two thousand years ago by the Brumas, cannibal witches who kept their prey alive and grew extra limbs on them to feed the tribe. The empires wiped them out fifteen hundred years later and kept the knowledge. Scholars think the Brumas were so far ahead that the field is still catching up. Today biomancy is a fast-growing, heavily regulated branch of medicine, and the regulation only binds the practitioners who agree to be bound.

Born different

Empaths

Some people are born able to feel the emotions of others. Gaining the ability through magic or bodily modification is highly illegal as a violation of privacy. Those born with it are not imprisoned, but they face heavy scrutiny to prove it, must swear an oath never to abuse it, and are often harassed and shunned anyway. Identifying and monitoring them takes enormous resources, and that matters more every year, because the number of people born with this and other, far more dangerous abilities is rising fast. Nobody knows why, and a great many people are worried about what it means.

Anomalies

An anomaly is born with talent so extreme and potential so nearly limitless that both are close to godlike. What makes them dangerous is the rate: they do not plateau. The more they use what they excel at, the faster they grow.

The first anomaly anyone names is Archmage Violet T. Fernand, who shaped how the world understands magic nine thousand years ago. She became an archmage at five. The world is still trying to catch up to what she knew, entire fields exist only to decode her writing, and some of it is still incomprehensible. It is said magic itself whispered to her.

The last anomaly to survive into adulthood killed four Archfey before the All Mother struck him down herself. Because of him, anomalies are now killed in their cribs, by the empires or by the Archfey. Perhaps one is born every thousand years. It is a taboo subject in many places, especially the part about what happens when one is born.

Immortals

More immortals exist than most people expect, given how hard it is to become one. The oldest on record is Angelia V. Martin, grand priestess of Hope, who became immortal roughly six thousand years ago during the Age of the Warring Kingdoms and has never told anyone how. The leading theory is the All Mother herself.

Many immortals are believed to live outside the public eye, and people hunt them for lost knowledge, for treasure, or for the secret itself, usually out of fear of whatever afterlife waits for them or for some unfinished business. The price is very heavy. Most who manage it end up as monsters who want to die.

Doorways

The fourteen divine realms connect to Mirrorbloom through doorways, some fixed and well mapped, others wandering. Crossing between Solance and Umbrasol is far harder and far more dangerous. See Crossing between halves and Courts & Realms.
