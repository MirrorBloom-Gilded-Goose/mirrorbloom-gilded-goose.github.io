The Sleeping Meadows

An endless meadow under a sky full of stars, where nearly every soul in Mirrorbloom goes to rest.

Arriving

Several afterlives exist, but one is so much larger than the rest that most people simply call it the Meadow. The Sleeping Meadows lie under the All Mother's domain, and nearly every being in Mirrorbloom goes there when they die.

Souls rest because they have to. By the end of a life, the weight of every memory and all the power a person has gathered presses on the soul, and it needs to put that weight down.

The intake is gentle and strange. You are cleaned, thoroughly, head to toe, and dried, and you feel cleaner than you have ever felt. As you are cleaned, a bone-chilling tiredness passes through you: a whole lifetime catching up at once. Then you are sent to bed, alone or with the people you love.

The long sleep

The church and the Knights of Hope both describe it as beautiful: millions of pods drifting along an infinite meadow beneath a starlit sky, each one holding a sleeper in the most comfortable bed they will ever lie in.

Every so often, a soul wakes. It walks out to one of the meadow's gathering places, meets the family and friends who passed before it, and then goes back to rest. The cycle repeats forever. How long a soul sleeps between wakings depends entirely on the soul: some wake after a few days, some after centuries. That is why a promise to meet someone in the Meadow can be honest and still impossible to keep on time.

The lost lambs

Not every soul settles. A few split away from the others and wander the Meadow instead of taking a bed. They are called lost lambs. Nobody knows why it happens to some souls and not others.

Judgment

Each soul is judged on its own merits. But the Law of Reflection reaches even here. Genuine goodness in one life quietly pushes its mirror self, on the other side of the glass, toward a darker fate. Nobody who truly understands the Meadow believes goodness comes free.

Other afterlives

Each of the twelve Archfey keeps a smaller afterlife of their own for their most devoted followers, shaped by that Archfey's nature and domain. They are real, and some are lovely, but none of them comes close to the Meadow in size.

Fear of what comes after drives a great deal of mortal behavior, including the hunt for immortals and their secrets.

The Gorge

Umbrasol has an afterlife too, and it belongs to Mistress Despair. Her church calls it the Gorge. Nothing about it has been confirmed by anyone who came back, but this is official doctrine:

The damned are packed into barrels, tightly, their bones broken to make them fit. Each barrel is filled with a black tar that eats away at body and soul alike while replaying the sleeper's worst fears. Slowly the barrel leaks their essence into jars, and the jars are used to fill Despair's cup, and the cups of her guests.

Who her guests are, the doctrine does not say.
