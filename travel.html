Roads, Skies & Tunnels

Three ways across the world, and three different answers to the question of who owns the way.

Trade in Mirrorbloom moves by land, by air and by water, and each route is governed differently. On the roads, empires license the guilds. In the open sky, nobody does. Under the sea, three kingdoms own every inch.

The roads

Surface roads belong to the nations they cross, and the nations hand them to trade guilds by license. A guild that holds a road charter keeps the road maintained and safe, and keeps the tolls. The three that matter most are the Ironroad Consortium, the Wayfarer's Charter and the Long Ledger Guild.

The skies

Above the roads runs the most important logistics system in the world: the global air currents, a wind system that circles the whole of Solance and regulates its climate. Specialized ships ride the currents from continent to continent faster than anything on the ground.

No empire holds any claim over the open sky. The sky guilds are independent, and they compete fiercely for the best currents and the best contracts.

Dominant
The Skyfarer's Concord
Rivals
Rustwing Freighters, the Cloudjackers, the Kite-and-Compass Guild, Featherfall Logistics, the Storm Chasers' Union and the Drifting Anchor

Because the currents carry the weather as well as the ships, anything that disrupted them would be catastrophic for the climate of the whole world. Sky captains know it. So, presumably, does anyone who has ever wanted to hold the world to ransom.

The tunnels

Beneath the ocean lies a massive aquatic world of underwater tunnels, home to the fish folk and to a legitimate trade route of its own. It is slower to equip for than the roads and far more expensive in gear, but it goes where nothing else can.

The tunnels are under full sovereign control of the three kingdoms on the plates beneath the sea: the Deepmarch Collective, the Abyssal Thrones and the Tidebound Kingdoms. Standard access is open to anyone who pays the toll. Anything more requires a treaty.

Ley line wells

Scattered across the world are ley line wells: ancient sites from before the Sundering where the Mana Field runs so dense and so wild that it cannot be harvested. They are far too volatile for that. Anything that lives near one mutates, chaotically and without direction.

The wells are guarded and regulated by the Kenanism Empire, which treats them as holy. One of them holds the fifteen islands of the Skyhold Dominion in the air.

Inside the cities

In the Shining Everlast the journey is vertical as much as horizontal. Monorail lines run between and through the towers, transit lifts connect the tiers, and rabbitfolk courier clans move anything small and urgent faster than either. See the Shining Everlast.
