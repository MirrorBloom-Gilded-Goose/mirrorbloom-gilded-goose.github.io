Player's Guide

Making a character in MirrorBloom. What they would know, where they could be from, who they pray to, and the laws that will get them arrested.

Everything on this page is common knowledge somewhere in Solance, the bright half of the world, where most characters are born. Nothing here is a secret your table will regret you reading. The deep truths stay with your Dungeon Master.

Reading Tales of the Gilded Goose too? Set the chapter you have reached and anything the serial reveals later is blurred.

What your character knows

Knowledge in this world comes in layers, like an iceberg, and going deeper changes the people who do. Most characters start at the surface. A character trained at a mages' college, or one with long experience of the world, can start one layer down. Anything below that is for play to uncover.

Everyone knows

Every child, above and below

  • The two goddesses exist: the Great Hope and the Great Despair.
  • Solance and Umbrasol mirror each other, and Umbrasol is the evil twin.
  • The Law of Reflection, as folklore: kindness above is paid for below.
  • The races, and that Kenanism is where most of them came from.
  • The Archfey are gods, and you pray to them.

The educated know

College-trained, or long experience

  • The Mana Field exists, and it reacts to what is done in it.
  • Rituals are illegal.

They do not know the real reason rituals are illegal, or how dangerous they are. Most never ask.

Where you are from

Solance holds thirteen nations across three continents, one dominion in the sky and three kingdoms beneath the sea. Below them sit hundreds of smaller kingdoms, city states and free territories, so your character's home can be somewhere nobody has written down yet. Ask your DM.

Your people

Mirrorbloom began with ten races, as nature made them. Today it has hundreds. Almost all of the others trace back to the Kenanism Empire, where pilgrims mutate themselves and, when a change satisfies them, go out and found a new bloodline somewhere else. Some of these peoples are thousands of years old; others are startlingly recent. So if you want to play something new, there is room for it. Work out with your DM where your bloodline began.

Glasswrights
A young species, about seven hundred years old, whose kingdom fell in the Great Umbrasol Siege. The survivors built a foothold in the Everlast.
Vampires
Few, long-lived and influential. Native vampires arose in Kenanism and hold the foreign kind in open contempt.
The Venstar
An offshoot of the vampires born of a Kenanism mutation: weaker than the original bloodline, but crueler. They raid by night in Stillhedge, and sightings are now reported all over the world.
Faeries
The working class of the Archfey's realms, now settling in Mirrorbloom in numbers. Businesses employ countless faeries to run their establishments the way the Fey Lords do.

Where you go matters as much as what you are. The Umbral Crown is the friendliest place for the newer races. Ironpeak is openly bigoted toward anyone who is not one of the original ten.

Who you pray to

Most people in Solance serve the All Mother or one of the Archfey, and every faith in the world traces its teachings to one of the Twelve Positions set down in the Second Age. A cleric's power comes from faith and from their god.

The Dawnward King The Verdant Lady The Revel King The Lady of Dreaming The Hearthkeeper The Hunter King

Each of them has a twin below the glass. People who worship the dark Fey Lords and Mistress Despair do exist, and they are hunted across the continent by the Marauders. Playing one is a conversation to have with your DM first.

The Kenanism Empire is a faith of its own: mutation is sacred there, and the ley line wells are holy ground.

Magic at the table

Every living being has a personal core that generates its own magic and refills on its own. Casters also draw on the Mana Field around them to fuel bigger workings. Magic takes the color of the soul that holds it, so no two casters' spells look quite alike: describe yours.

Wizards
Small reserves, a great deal of intelligence, a spell stockpiled for everything.
Witches
Much the same, but focused on ritual and the darker arts.
Clerics
Power drawn from faith and from their god.
Sorcerers
The lucky ones: huge reserves and good instincts, and everyone else's jealousy.

A new kind of magic is discovered almost every year, and very few catch on. See the Kinds of Magic for necromancy, frost, time, wild magic, biomancy and the rest.

The law

  • Rituals are illegal across Solance. Deliberately injecting magic into the Mana Field by ritual will get you arrested anywhere.
  • Harrow's Law. In the capital of the Shining Everlast, casting in public needs a license, and every single casting is logged.
  • Mutating yourself on purpose is outlawed in most nations. Kenanism built a religion on it.
  • Gaining empathy by magic is highly illegal. People born empaths must prove it and swear an oath never to abuse it.
  • Biomancy is heavily regulated medicine. The regulation only binds the practitioners who agree to be bound.

Hazards

  • Dead zones zap away cast magic inside them. Your core keeps refilling, so you get your magic back almost as soon as you walk out.
  • Ley line wells mutate anything that lives near them, chaotically and without direction.
  • Overloading your body with mana makes you stronger and faster for a moment, then almost always kills or mutates you. Out of a million recorded attempts, two have succeeded.

Your reflection

Everyone born in Mirrorbloom is bound to a mirror self in Umbrasol, whose heart runs opposite to their own. Scholars call it the Shackle. When one half truly changes, in intent rather than in act, the other shifts to keep the balance. Most people live and die without meeting theirs. Some spend their whole lives trying to. A few, the unlucky ones, are found first.

Your character has one. What they know about it, and whether it knows about them, is a good thing to settle with your DM.

Leaving your mark

If your character grew up in the Everlast, they grew up with its national dream: to leave your mark, a trace in the living Mana Field that remembers a life lived well. Every calling has its own road to it, which makes it a ready-made motivation.

Mages, engineers, artificers
Invent and discover.
Bards
Immortalize stories and music.
Warriors
Earn deeds worth retelling.
Druids
Keep the Mana Field and the natural world healthy, and seek no glory for it.
Rangers
Preserve wildlife.
Rogues
Take the path in reverse: become a legend whispered through secrecy and rumor.
Clerics
Spread faith and shared joy until they are remembered almost as saints.
Everyone else
Pride in their craft. The best bread in the district.

The year turns on four festivals: Marking Day, the Illuminance, Unity Week and Founding Day. All four are on Life in the Everlast.

Getting around

Roads
Belong to the nations they cross, which license trade guilds to keep them safe and collect the tolls.
Skies
Ships ride the global air currents between continents, faster than anything on the ground. No empire owns the sky; the sky guilds fight over it.
Tunnels
Beneath the sea, open to anyone who pays the toll. Anything more needs a treaty.
Cities
In the Everlast, monorails, transit lifts and rabbitfolk couriers.

Not said in polite company

Things your character can bring up, as long as they are ready for the looks.

  • The Law of Reflection. Everyone knows the folklore. Actually studying it is outlawed, and most people never wonder why.
  • Anomalies. A taboo subject in many places, especially what happens when one is born.
  • Empaths. Even with the oath sworn, born empaths are often harassed and shunned.
  • Immortals. People hunt them for lost knowledge, for treasure, or for the secret itself.
  • Umbrasol. Crossing between the halves is far harder and far more dangerous than visiting the Archfey's realms.
