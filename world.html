The Thirteen Nations

The Thirteen Nations

Three continents, one nation in the sky and three beneath the sea. Every one of them is cast again below the glass.

Solance holds thirteen nations, spread across three continents, a dominion in the sky and the deep kingdoms under the sea. Each has its twin in Umbrasol, with the same name and the same peoples, inverted.

Below the level of nations sit hundreds of smaller kingdoms, city states and free territories. A few of them, like Vaelgard and Kelvarion, matter far more than their size suggests.

Solmarch

The first continent, and the heart of the story

Solmarch is where most of what is written here takes place. It holds the greatest empire in the world, the oldest one, and the strangest one, all sharing a single continent and keeping a careful eye on each other. Kenanism sits far out on Solmarch's long eastern reach, well away from the Everlast.

The Shining EverlastEmpire of Hopelight

The largest and most powerful nation in Mirrorbloom: one unbroken city of towers and some fifty million people, governed by a Senate that has existed for only about a hundred and twenty years. Its mirror below is the Last Shine. See also Life in the Everlast.

The Yenfin EmpireThe first empire

A conservative monarchy, a true golden-age power during the War a hundred and twenty years ago, and now faded, under-respected and losing people to emigration. Its newest emperor is fifteen.

The Kenanism EmpireWhere mutation is sacred

An empire built around a mana leyline whose people deliberately mutate themselves in pursuit of the perfect being. Its pilgrims are the reason Mirrorbloom has hundreds of races.

The Umbral Crown

The second continent

Despite a name that sounds like it belongs below the glass, the Umbral Crown is a continent of Solance. Its three nations are known for accepting the mutation-descended races more readily than almost anywhere else, which makes them a common destination for Kenanism pilgrims looking for somewhere to found a bloodline.

The third continent

Unnamed in most records; some call it Stillhedge

The third continent keeps to itself. Its nations are closed off and low on magic, and they are home only to the original ten races, as nature made them, with none of the hundreds that came after.

IronpeakThe mountain empire

A huge mountain empire of only the original ten races, and fiercely proud of it, to the point of open bigotry toward everyone else. It was founded by an elf, Von Iron, who grew tired of the strangeness on the other continents and sailed across to build an empire of the ten races as he believed they were supposed to be. Being an elf, he is still alive, and still rules.

Its nobility is classic and rigid. The great houses descend from those who sailed with him; the minor houses are people he raised up later. Peasants are looked down on. Otherwise it runs much as a medieval kingdom would, Adventurers Guild included. Magic is uncommon and somewhat looked down on, though not outright distrusted.

Visitors from the Everlast often find it freer: more privacy, fewer rules. But progress is stifled in its own way. The culture is deeply traditional, and casters stick to standard spells. Underneath runs a seedy underbelly of corrupt nobles and an illegal magic scene of underground learners, spells smuggled in from the Everlast and illicit experiments, and because it is repressed, it pushes further and more dangerously than anything in the Everlast. Not every noble is corrupt; some sincerely want less magic in the world, mostly warrior and cleric types.

The Skyhold Dominion

One nation, fifteen islands, no ground

Fifteen islands hang in the sky above Solance, held aloft by a single ley line well they all share. Together they form one unified federal nation, with each island running its own local affairs under a federal government that speaks for all fifteen. Whatever happens to the well happens to every island at once, which Skyhold's politicians are careful never to say out loud.

The kingdoms beneath the sea

Three nations on the plates under the ocean

Beneath the ocean floor, on the tectonic plates themselves, sit three underground kingdoms. Between them they control the underwater tunnels, a vast aquatic world and one of the three great trade routes of Mirrorbloom. Passage is legitimate and open for a toll; anything more needs a treaty. See Roads, Skies & Tunnels.

Kingdoms worth knowing

VaelgardThe warrior kingdom

A small kingdom that rivals a small empire. Every man, woman and child is a warrior first and a tradesperson second, and the kingdom has a deep affinity for frost magic. It was not always so. During the Great Umbrasol Siege the king watched Vaelgard nearly overrun for the fifth time, armed everyone, and swore it would never be pushed around again. Vaelgard became one of the six great key points in breaking the invasion.

Its reflection below is warrior-coded too, but cowardly and backstabbing, a court where a blade in the back counts as tactics.

KelvarionThe debt never paid

When Umbrasol besieged the Shining Everlast a hundred and twenty years ago, Kelvarion sent reinforcements and flooded the invaders out. The Everlast never repaid that debt, thanks to corrupt and overreaching politicians, and Kelvarion has not forgotten.

Peoples

Mirrorbloom began with ten races, as nature made them. Today it has hundreds. Almost all of the others trace back to the Kenanism Empire, where pilgrims mutate themselves and, when a change satisfies them, go out and found a new bloodline somewhere else. Some of these peoples are thousands of years old. Others are startlingly recent.

Glasswrights
A young species, about seven hundred years old, descended from Sandra P. Won, who mutated herself in Kenanism and then broke with custom by settling on the outskirts of the Everlast while it was still an elves-only empire. Her family became a kingdom ruled by her descendants. It fell in the Great Umbrasol Siege, and the survivors fled into the Everlast, where they have built a comfortable foothold, a long way from what they lost.
Vampires
Few in number and great in influence, because they live so long. There is more than one kind. Most came from other realms, Vesper Nightfall among them. Native vampires arose in Kenanism about five hundred years ago: more mutated, less human, missing many of the foreign kind's weaknesses, and on better terms with everyone else. They hold foreign vampires in open contempt for ruining their image, which has caused a few minor wars. Vesper has been making real headway in closing that gap.
Faeries
The working class of the Archfey's realms, who strengthen the barriers between realms and cook for the Fey Lords and their guests. Their mischief has melded with a fierce work ethic, which is why trickster toys flooded the market when they began settling in Mirrorbloom. Businesses now employ countless faeries to run their establishments the way the Fey Lords do, and a whole younger generation is migrating here for a better life.
