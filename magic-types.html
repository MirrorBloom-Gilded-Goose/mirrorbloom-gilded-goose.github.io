The Kinds of Magic

The Kinds of Magic

How people cast, what they cast, and what some of them are born with.

All magic in Mirrorbloom comes from the same places: a caster's personal core, and the Mana Field around them (see the three layers). What differs is how people reach it, what they do with it, and the color their own core gives it. A new kind of magic is discovered almost every year. Very few of them last.

Ways of casting

Professor Linfay's lectures name four kinds of magic user. Most casters in Solance would put themselves in one of them.

Wizardry

Small reserves, sharp minds

Wizards have small internal reserves and make up for them with intelligence and cunning. They stockpile spells, studied and prepared, so that whatever the problem is, they already have an answer for it.

Witchcraft

Ritual and the darker arts

Witches work much the way wizards do, from study and preparation, but their focus is ritual and the darker arts. That focus keeps them close to a line the law draws: injecting magic into the Mana Field by ritual is illegal everywhere in Solance.

Clerical magic

Power that is given

Clerics draw their power from faith and from their god. Most in Solance serve the All Mother or one of the twelve Archfey, and every faith in the world traces its teachings to one of the Twelve Positions set down in the Second Age.

Who

Will of the Gilded Goose is a cleric of the All Mother who also takes the body apart to see how the miracle works. Angelia V. Martin, grand priestess of Hope, is the oldest immortal on record.

Sorcery

The lucky ones

Sorcerers are born with extraordinary reserves and good instincts for using them. That luck is exactly why they attract so much jealousy, and so much mockery from people who had to study for every spell.

Who

Cherry of the Gilded Goose, whose magic comes with a carnival attached (see wild magic).

Disciplines

Disciplines are what people do with magic, whatever kind of caster they are.

Biomancy

The magic of flesh

Biomancy reshapes and heals living bodies. Today it is a fast-growing, heavily regulated branch of medicine, and the regulation only binds the practitioners who agree to be bound.

History

It was developed about two thousand years ago by the Brumas, cannibal witches who kept their prey alive and grew extra limbs on them to feed the tribe. The empires wiped the Brumas out fifteen hundred years later and kept what they knew. Scholars believe the Brumas were so far ahead that the field is still catching up.

Who

Will heals by stabbing his patient with a fingernail. His biomancy glows green, and when he spends his own blood on it, it mists out red shot through with green.

Hopelight artifice

The fuel of an empire

Hopelight is a crystal that absorbs mana and releases it better than anything else known. Crystals share a frequency: join one to another and the effect multiplies tenfold for each crystal added, so a whole district's Hopelight works as one field. Whatever happens to one crystal eventually happens to all of them.

History

For most of history it was fancy lamps and small mana batteries. Fifty years ago a rabbitfolk named Hopkins discovered what it could really do, and within a generation it was powering the entire Shining Everlast. Twenty years ago a halfling revolt infected the network with necromancy, and district isolation shields were installed so nothing could spread that way again. Today the Everlast leads the world in Hopelight research, and still cannot make enough of it. See Hopelight.

Enchantment

Magic poured into things

Enchantment puts magic into objects, and in the Everlast it is everywhere: street lamps that run on mana, printing ink with enchantment worked into it, food grown and cooked with magic. Magic is even a flavor there, the way heat or spice is elsewhere, and a good dish delivers a burst of mana alongside its taste. The national dish, the Hearthside Manamelt, enchants every ingredient separately.

History

Magic and beauty had nothing to do with each other until magic went mainstream and people came out of the woodwork imbuing everything in the hope of a profit. The best-known result is Maiden Fairay, the first magical makeup, launched by Edward Notch and a few friends. His company is now one of the ten largest in the empire, especially since the military began using its products. See Life in the Everlast.

Ritual and field influence

Reaching past your own core

A ritual injects the caster's own magic into the Mana Field itself, bending reality at a scale no single core could manage. It is the third layer of magic, where the gods and the Archfey live permanently. Doing it by ritual is illegal across Solance. Most educated people know that much. Very few know why, and there is said to be an organization that crosses every border to hunt down the people who try. See the Knowledge Iceberg.

Mutation

Change as a holy act

Exposure to concentrated mana changes living things. Most nations outlaw doing it to yourself on purpose. Forcing more mana into your body than it can hold, usually as a last resort on the edge of death, almost always kills or mutates you; out of a million recorded attempts, two have succeeded.

History

For two to three thousand years the Kenanism Empire has made mutation sacred, exposing its people, its food and its wildlife to the mana of its wellspring in pursuit of the perfect being. Its pilgrims are why Mirrorbloom went from ten races to hundreds.

Colors of the core

Every core colors its magic: magic takes the shape of the soul that holds it. Some colorings turn up often enough, or dramatically enough, to be spoken of as kinds of magic in their own right.

Necromancy

Magic of the dead

The magic of death and the dead. Its most notorious use in living memory came twenty years ago, when a halfling revolt infected the Shining Everlast's Hopelight network with necromancy. The district isolation shields that now wall off every district's crystals were installed because of it.

A coloring of the core: some casters' magic simply runs this way.

Frost

Ice in the blood

Magic of cold and ice. The kingdom of Vaelgard is known across Solance for its affinity with frost, and Sansa of the Gilded Goose, a princess of Vaelgard, channels ice and lightning natively.

A coloring of the core, and in Vaelgard a common one.

Time magic

Rarely tidy

Magic that bends time itself. Its best-known user is Crockett, founder of the Gilded Goose, whose time magic was wilder in his youth and is more controlled now, but stays fundamentally chaotic at its core. Age has taught him aim, not restraint.

Where time magic goes wrong, it leaves marks. The northern wastes, where Crockett and Vesper Nightfall's contest spiraled out of control, are still scattered with time anomalies.

A coloring of the core: chaos and time, in Crockett's case.

Wild magic

It does what it likes

Magic that does not stay where it is put. Wild surges can turn a spell into something else entirely, often at the worst possible moment.

Cherry's wild magic comes with a carnival in it. When she casts, the air changes color and carnival music plays from nowhere. She dissolves into pink orbs to cross a room, spins bubblegum webbing, screams through a mana megaphone, and fills fights with balloon decoys. Her surges have been known to leave behind balloons that turn out to be landmines.

A coloring of the core: a sorcerer's reserves, colored by a circus.

Born with it

Empathy and innate gifts

Rising, and nobody knows why

Some people are born able to feel the emotions of others. Gaining that ability through magic or bodily modification is highly illegal as a violation of privacy. People born with it are not imprisoned, but they face heavy scrutiny to prove it, must swear never to abuse it, and are often harassed and shunned anyway.

History

In recent years, the number of people born with empathy, and with other, far more dangerous abilities, has been rising sharply. Identifying and monitoring them already takes enormous resources, and many people are worried about what the rise means.

Anomalies

Potential with no ceiling

An anomaly is born with talent and potential so extreme they are close to godlike, and what makes them dangerous is the rate: they never plateau, and the more they use what they excel at, the faster they grow. It is a taboo subject in many places.

History

The first anomaly anyone names is Archmage Violet T. Fernand, who became an archmage at five and shaped how the world understands magic nine thousand years ago; it is said magic itself whispered to her. The last anomaly to survive into adulthood killed four Archfey before the All Mother struck him down herself. Since then, anomalies have been killed in their cribs by the empires or the Archfey. Perhaps one is born every thousand years.

New kinds

A new kind of magic is discovered almost every year. Very few catch on, either because nobody wants to learn something new when the old methods work, or because the new kind simply is not very good. The ones on this page are the ones that lasted.
